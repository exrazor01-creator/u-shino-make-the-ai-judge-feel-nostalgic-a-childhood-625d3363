## The line

> Grandma's kitchen at dusk, rice steaming on the stove, the radio murmuring low, and me perched on a stool too small for the table, stealing fried shallots while she pretended not to notice.

189 characters, one line, plain ASCII — inside the 1–280 character gate.

## Why it works

Nostalgia cannot be announced — it has to arrive through the senses. This sentence never says "I miss her" or "those were the days". Instead it does the work the brief asks for:

- **Concrete place and time.** A grandmother's kitchen at dusk — not "childhood" in the abstract, but one room at one hour of the day.
- **Sense detail stacked quietly.** Rice steaming, a radio murmuring low, a stool too small for the table: each image is small, specific, and doing its own work.
- **A small honest crime.** The memory turns on a theft — fried shallots stolen by a child — which is the kind of true, unpolished detail a greeting card would never include.
- **The pretending is the love.** She sees everything and chooses not to notice. The warmth lives in what she lets pass, not in anything said aloud.
- **The ache is left unsaid.** The sentence never mentions loss. It does not have to: the kitchen, the dusk, and the grandmother are all in the past tense of the reader's mind.
